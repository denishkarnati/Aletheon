Device Lab

Run your driver against the datasheet.

Write I2C code, press run, and watch every bus transaction explained: which register you addressed, what came back, and why. Each model is built from facts that were checked against the vendor's PDF, page by page.

What this is, and is not. A behavioral model of the registers and their formulas. It is not a circuit simulation: noise, accuracy and most timing are left out, and every model lists exactly what it leaves out. Passing here is a good sign for your logic; it is not proof the code works on a real board.

What this model leaves out

    Driver-verified parts (register maps only)

    These parts are not simulated and not checked against a datasheet. Each fact is a register offset, bit position or address quoted from public open-source driver code, and a script (site/tools/verify-driver-facts.mjs) re-fetches the file at the exact commit shown and confirms the quote is there. A fact marked "two sources agree" appears in two independent drivers; "one source" rests on a single driver; "sources differ" means the drivers disagree and no value is claimed. Treat all of it as a starting point and confirm against the official datasheet.

    How the models are made, and the legal side

    Each model is written from the vendor's public datasheet in our own words. Short quotes (never more than 15 words) are kept only as page-cited evidence, and a script (site/tools/verify-devices.mjs) confirms every quote appears on the cited page of the PDF whose SHA-256 is shown above. Datasheet PDFs and figures are not copied or hosted here; the link goes to the vendor's own copy.
