Scenario Labs

Fix it like on a real bench.

Five short, hands-on problems that firmware engineers actually meet. Each one runs on the same models as the rest of the platform, can be solved, and fails the obvious wrong way for a reason you can see. They link out to real boards in the Atlas, real parts in the catalog, the datasheet-checked models in the Device Lab, the IoT Bench and the Logic Analyzer.

What this is, and is not. Each lab teaches a general lesson using behavioral models. The numbers (sleep current, radio bursts, battery curve) are illustrative assumptions you can change, not measurements of any product. A lab passing here means you understood the idea, not that a design is ready for hardware.

1 · Build a battery weather node

Goal: a node with a TMP117 air-temperature sensor and an ADS1115 reading a soil probe must report at least once a minute and run for at least two years on its battery. Pick a board, a radio and a battery, and watch where the charge goes.

What these labs leave out

  • Real hardware: noise, tolerances, temperature, layout and the many ways a prototype fails that a model cannot show.
  • Any specific product's current draw. Every power figure is an assumption you can change; look up the real numbers in the datasheet of the part you use.
  • The MCU running firmware. A lab can show that a design is plausible; it cannot replace flashing a board and measuring it.
