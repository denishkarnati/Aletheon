IoT Bench

A fleet of sensor nodes, in your browser.

Each virtual node wakes on a timer, reads real register-level models of a TI sensor over a virtual I²C bus, spends charge from a battery, and publishes to an MQTT-style broker. Break things on purpose: drop packets, drift a sensor, unplug it, brown the supply out, then watch what the dashboard sees.

What this is, and is not. The sensor parts (TMP117, INA219, INA226, ADS1115) are the same datasheet-checked register models as the Device Lab. Everything around them is a behavioral model with numbers you choose: current draw, battery curve, radio bursts, link loss and the "world" the sensors measure are assumptions, not measurements of any product. The broker follows MQTT's topic, QoS 0/1, retained-message and last-will rules, but there is no TCP, TLS, QoS 2 or persistent session. No noise or analog error is modeled. Use it to learn how a design behaves, then confirm on real hardware.

Power budget

A duty-cycled node sleeps almost all the time, so the average is a weighted mix of three states. Fill in your own numbers, or take the selected node's. The simulation above spends charge the same way, so the two agree when nothing goes wrong; retries, brown-out reboots and the accelerator make the running node spend more.

What this bench leaves out

  • Noise, accuracy and temperature drift of the sensors themselves (the only drift is the fault you inject). The part models are exact register arithmetic.
  • Bus timing at the clock-edge level: conversion time is modeled, but not SCL clock edges (the Analyzer lays those out from the byte log).
  • The radio: no path loss, collisions, TCP retransmission, TLS handshakes or reconnect storms. "Loss" drops whole messages.
  • MQTT QoS 2, persistent sessions, authentication, message size limits and broker clustering.
  • The battery's real discharge curve, temperature and ageing: voltage is a straight line from full to empty with a fixed series resistance.
  • The MCU itself: its current is a number you type, not the result of running firmware.
