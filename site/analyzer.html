Logic Analyzer

See the bus. Read the bytes.

Every transaction from the Device Lab and the IoT Bench can be drawn as the clock and data lines it would put on a wire, then decoded back by a separate decoder. Step through a NACK, change the SPI mode and watch the data shift, set the wrong baud rate, or import a CSV from a real logic analyzer.

What this is, and is not. Ideal digital levels: edges are instant and clean. It does not model rise times, ringing, glitches, bus capacitance, pull-up values, clock stretching or whether real hardware would meet its timing. When a transaction comes from the Bench or Lab, the waveform is generated from the byte log, and the decoder reading it back is a check that the two agree. Imported CSVs are decoded as they are.

Source

CSV from a logic analyzer: a header row (Time [s],SCL,SDA), then one row per change with the time in seconds and a 0 or 1 per channel. Up to 200,000 rows. It stays in your browser; nothing is uploaded.

What it found

Try "Debug a NACK": the first transaction goes to 0x49, nothing answers, and the SDA line stays high on the ninth clock. The part is strapped to answer at 0x48.

Decoder

The generic device is invented for teaching: command 0x03 reads, 0x02 writes, then an address, then data. It is not any real part. Set the decoder to a different mode than the bus and the bytes shift or flip, which is exactly what a wrong CPOL/CPHA looks like on a real board.

What this analyzer leaves out

  • Everything analog: edges are ideal, there is no noise, glitch, ringing, slow rise from a weak pull-up, or threshold voltage.
  • I²C clock stretching, arbitration, 10-bit addresses, the general-call address and multi-master buses. The decoder reads 7-bit addressing.
  • SPI chips with more than one chip select, daisy chains, dual and quad modes, and word sizes other than 8 bits.
  • UART with RS-232 voltage levels, flow control, 9-bit frames and automatic baud detection.
  • Timing checks: nothing here tells you that your bus meets a part's setup, hold or rise-time limits. Use the datasheet and a real analyzer for that.
